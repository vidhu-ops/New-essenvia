1. Home

The first screen. Next up is the household question.

Why it's here. A visitor needs to know what Essentra is within about ten seconds. So the page has one sentence, one picture of the box and one clear button. No catalogue, no pop-ups.

What it does for Essentra. The aim is that people go straight into building a box instead of leaving to compare. The brand names in the picture answer the first worry, which is "will I recognise what I get?"

Why we expect it to work. Familiar names are meant to build trust quickly, so Tide, Charmin and Bounty should help the first impression. Only two buttons (start, or learn more) is meant to keep the choice easy.

2. Household

Comes after Home. Next up is laundry.

Why it's here. It's the easiest question we can ask. One tap, no typing, nothing to look up. Starting small takes away the fear of a long form.

What it does for Essentra. The answer gives us a first guess for every amount that follows, so later questions can come pre-filled and stay short.

Why we expect it to work. The idea is that a small first yes makes the next one feel natural. The progress bar is there to show how close the end is, which we hope keeps people moving.

3. Laundry (and the other categories)

Comes after Household. Paper, kitchen and cleaning work the same way, then we reach the package.

Why it's here. This is where the customer builds their box. We ask about habits, like loads per week, because people know how they live. They don't know our product sizes.

What it does for Essentra. Behind the scenes we turn those habits into sizes and quantities and work out the cost to pick a package. The customer never sees costs, credits or add-ons.

Why we expect it to work. People tend to value things they helped build, so picking their own brands is meant to make the box feel like theirs. We'd expect that to help with the price, and we'll check. Big tiles keep choices few and easy to tap.

4. Your package

The big moment. Comes after the questions, leads to checkout.

Why it's here. The price only appears now, after the customer has spent a few minutes and can see their own products. It's the biggest thing on the screen and sits right above the main button.

What it does for Essentra. One flat monthly price means nothing to work out and nothing to question. Showing a real delivery date makes the offer feel close.

Why we expect it to work. Showing their products first is meant to make the price feel like a fair swap. A monthly figure is intended to feel lighter than a yearly one, and a matched package should be simpler than a menu. Real conversion data will show which of these hold up.

5. Checkout

Comes after the package. Leads to the confirmation.

Why it's here. This is where interest turns into a subscription. We only ask for what delivery needs, show the price and tax once, and put the monthly payment wording right beside the button.

What it does for Essentra. Clear terms and a visible security note should mean fewer people drop out and fewer disputes later. Stripe takes the card details, so they never touch our servers.

Why we expect it to work. Payment is where many people hesitate. A named payment company, a security line and a plain cancellation note are designed to answer two quiet questions: is this safe, and can I leave? Being open about monthly billing is intended to cut down "I didn't know" disputes.

6. Confirmed

Comes after checkout. Leads to the dashboard.

Why it's here. It finishes the purchase on a good note and says exactly what happens next, so nobody is left wondering.

What it does for Essentra. Telling people which messages to expect should mean fewer "where's my order?" calls. It also asks them to set up an account while they're still keen.

7. Customer dashboard

Comes after confirmation. This is home for every delivery after that.

Why it's here. The next delivery sits at the top because it's the reason people come back. Skip, pause and change are one tap away.

What it does for Essentra. Skip and pause are kept prominent as lighter options, and cancel is just as easy to find and clearly explained. The aim is that people adjust when they need to. Cut-off dates set in admin protect the warehouse.

Why we expect it to work. Feeling in control is meant to make people comfortable staying. Skip and pause sit up front as lighter options, and cancel stays clear and easy to find. We'd expect some people to take the smaller step, but that's for the data to show, and nobody should feel pushed.

8. Rep: compare

First screen of the door-to-door flow. Leads on to customize, package and customer details.

Why it's here. A rep needs a way to start the conversation. Putting the customer's current routine next to Essentra sets up the offer before anyone mentions a price.

What it does for Essentra. Every rep opens the same way, and honestly. The prices come from admin, never scraped from stores, and reps can't see wholesale cost.

Why we expect it to work. We judge value against something, so showing today's routine first is intended to make one price and zero trips easy to weigh up. A clean tablet should also help the rep look professional at the door.

9. Rep: sign and pay

Last screen. After it, the receipt, account and first order are made for us.

Why it's here. It ends the sale where it began. Price, billing and cancellation sit on the left. The signature and card tap sit on the right. One big button comes last.

What it does for Essentra. One tap sets up the account, the subscription, the first payment, the saved card token, the consent record, the first order and the rep's credit for the sale.

Why we expect it to work. Price and terms sit on the same screen as the signature so there are no surprises, which is meant to reduce later cancellations. A signature and a card tap are deliberate acts and should make the decision feel clear.

Colour: four options for a Canadian household brand

The first version used blue only. Blue works, but it's also what every bank in Canada uses, and half the soap aisle is blue too. Here are three other directions, plus the blue for comparison. Each one has a quiet base, one main colour for buttons and prices, and a warm accent used very sparingly.

Lake Our pick

Lake #19707A
Ink #11303A
Slate
Mist
Amber

Why: teal sits between blue and green. It keeps blue's sense of trust and adds green's sense of safe, clean and healthy. It also looks like water, which suits laundry and dishes. Few banks or soap brands own it, so Essentra won't look like one of them.

The warm amber is there for small friendly touches like a tag or a highlight. On a cool base it adds a feeling of home, which helps in a country with long grey winters.

Why: deep green is the colour most linked to safe, natural and well looked after. It's an easy Canadian fit too, with forests, pine and the outdoors all in it. Gold brings in warmth, a bit like prairie fields.

Watch for: green is also used for "paid" and "success". We'd lean on words and icons for status so the brand colour doesn't get mixed up with it.

A fair warning about colour research. The studies agree on a few broad links (blue with trust, green with nature and health, red with urgency), but results change with culture, the product and what's next to it on the shelf. Colour helps, it doesn't decide. Our advice is to pick Lake, test it against Spruce with real customers on the home page, and keep the colour the same everywhere once chosen.

How the colours get used

ColourWhere it goes

Main colourButtons, links, selected tiles and prices. It's kept scarce so people learn it means "tap here". White text on it passes the AA accessibility test in all four palettes.

Type, layout and parts

These are the choices the screens are built from. We want a household brand that feels established and calm, with nothing on screen that doesn't help someone finish.

RuleWhat it means
One job per screenOne question or one decision, and one filled button. People will often be on a phone or a doorstep tablet.
Show their things firstTheir brands and amounts come before the price, so the price is easy to judge.
Say it plainlyShort sentences, real dates, no exclamation marks. This handles money and a front door, so calm wording builds trust.
Keep costs out of sightWholesale cost lives in admin only. Customers see one price and staff see only what their role needs.
AreaDecision
TypeA rounded serif for headings and prices (Literata), and Public Sans for everything else. Both are free to use, so no font fees. Body text is 16px so phones don't zoom into form fields. The rep tablet runs 2px larger.
Spacing and shape8px steps. Margins of 20px on phone, 32px on tablet and 64px on desktop, with content capped at 1,120px. Corners are 10px on inputs, 12px on buttons and tiles, and 14px on cards. Flat surfaces with 1px borders, and one soft shadow only on things that float.
TouchTargets are 48px on phone and 56px on tablet. Reps tap in cold and glare.
Buttons and tilesOne filled button per screen, with the outlined one for the second-best action. Labels say what happens ("Start my subscription"), never just "Submit". Tiles are used for short choices because they suit a thumb.
FormsThe label sits above the field, never only as placeholder text. Errors say what to fix, right beside the field.
Photos and iconsReal products on white, soft light, labels facing the camera. The hero is one open box with six to eight recognizable products and no stock families. Icons are simple line icons in navy or the main colour.
MotionQuick (about 200 ms). The progress bar slides, tiles press in slightly. No autoplay, parallax or timers, and the reduced-motion setting is respected.
AccessibilityOur target is WCAG 2.1 AA, which we build to either way. That is separate from the legal requirement, which depends on where and how Essentra operates (Ontario's AODA, for example, applies by organization size and names its own standard). Essentra's lawyer confirms what applies. Visible focus ring on every control, text that scales to 200%, and errors that screen readers announce.
PackagingA box in the main colour with a white wordmark, dark tape, and an insert card with the review QR code and the referral card. Delivery emails use the same coloured strip at the top.

The rules behind the screens

The screens are only half of Essentra. A subscription runs on rules, and those rules decide whether it feels smooth to run or turns into a daily headache. We suggest settling them in a short planning phase before the build starts, and agreeing each one with Essentra there.

Deliveries

Customers
